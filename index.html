Surds and radical expressions

A surd is a root that cannot be written exactly as a rational number. √2, √3 and √5 are surds; √4 is not, because it simplifies to 2.

Simplifying

Take out the largest square factor: √72 = √(36 × 2) = 6√2.

Rationalising the denominator

Multiply above and below by the surd, or by the conjugate where the denominator is a sum: 1 / (3 + √2) × (3 − √2)/(3 − √2) = (3 − √2) / 7.

Practice

Simplify √50, √98 and √242. Rationalise 5/√3 and 2/(1 + √5). Working is loaded with the exercises below.
